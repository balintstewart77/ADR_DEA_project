# Stage 2: assigning diagnostic families

A one-page guide for adjudicators. Stage 2 comes after the source reveal: you
now know which source gave which option, and you record **why the sources
disagree**. The definitions are the preregistered ones (protocol §9.3). Rules
are cited by the IDs in [taxonomy_rule_reference.md](taxonomy_rule_reference.md).

## Three questions, in order

1. **Is there anything to diagnose?** If the disagreement is fully explained
   and nothing is wrong, close with *No assignable issue* and give a short
   positive reason. If you cannot tell, that is not "no issue": record family 8.
2. **Where does the disagreement come from?** The register entry, the rules,
   a genuinely two-sided case, a source, the data or form, or owner knowledge.
   That choice is the family.
3. **For a source finding, is there a clear basis?** Disagreeing with the
   other sources, or with you, is never enough on its own.

## The eight families

| # | Family | Use when | Not when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Apparent model rule-application problem** | The model's option breaches an explicit rule, has materially weaker support than a displayed alternative, or omits a materially better-supported label, **on the evidence the model had**. | The rules or the entry do not settle it (use 4, 6 or 3). |
| 2 | **Apparent scratch-coder rule-application problem** | A coder's option breaches an explicit rule or applies the instructions inconsistently. Name the coder or coders. | Only the coders disagree with each other on a judgement call (use 6). |
| 3 | **Evidence problem** | The public entry is insufficient, ambiguous, misleading or too sparse to tell the options apart, so the sources reasonably guessed differently. | The entry is clear but the rules are not (use 4). |
| 4 | **Taxonomy problem** | A category is missing or poorly represented, rules overlap or contradict each other, or a boundary is ambiguous in the rules themselves. | The entry is too thin to apply clear rules (use 3). |
| 5 | **Project-knowledge gap** | An owner's judgement relies on information not in the public entry. Only where owner evidence is shown. | It is used to redefine what a register-only source should have inferred. |
| 6 | **Legitimate boundary case** | More than one option is defensible under the rules and the visible evidence. | One option is clearly better supported (consider 1 or 2). |
| 7 | **Data or instrument problem** | A parsed field, cleaning decision, Record ID, form design or branching rule affected a classification. | The data are fine and the judgement is hard. |
| 8 | **Unresolved** | The evidence does not support a confident diagnosis. | You are merely unsure which of two diagnoses fits; record both. |

## What each finding asks

* **Clear basis**, for families 1 and 2 only: *conflicts with an explicit
  rule*; *materially weaker support than a displayed alternative* (model
  only); *omits a materially better-supported label* (model only); *applies
  the instructions inconsistently* (coders only).
* **Rests on a Stage 1 conflict?** If you recorded the rule conflict blind at
  Stage 1, cite it. Its components, labels and reason carry over and are not
  asked again. A blind basis is stronger than one noticed after the reveal.
* **Mechanism**, for families 1, 2, 4, 6 and 7: choose from the searchable
  list, or *New mechanism* with a short name. Recurrence is counted by
  mechanism, so reuse one where it fits.
* **Release implication:** you nominate the case; you do not decide release.
  Release concerns the model's classifications, so a coder finding is usually
  *Caveat only*, or *None* if no rule was involved. *Evidence for prompt revision*, *taxonomy revision* or
  *non-release* each trigger a mandatory second review, so choose them only
  when you mean it, and say what you propose.

## Rules of thumb

* **Several families can apply.** Record one finding per family: for example
  a thin entry (3) plus a model that asserted a confident label the entry
  cannot support (1).
* **When sources converge against a rule, consider the rule.** If the model
  and most of the coders depart from a rule in the same direction, ask whether
  the rule, rather than each source, is the problem, and if so add a taxonomy
  finding (4). Where two rules pull in different directions there is no clear
  basis in the frozen rules, so a source finding (1 or 2) does not apply.
* **An error an unclear rule invited is two findings:** the source finding,
  and a separate taxonomy finding (4).
* **Uncertainty is an answer.** Family 8, or an honest evidence rating, is
  better than a forced diagnosis.
