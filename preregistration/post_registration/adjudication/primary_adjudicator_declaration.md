# Primary adjudicator declaration

Protocol §9.1 requires the primary adjudicator's identity, role in developing
the taxonomy, classifier or dashboard, and any other relevant conflicts of
interest to be recorded before adjudication begins. This is that record. Its
commit date is the evidence that it predates the first formal record.

Date: 2026-09-24

## Identity

Balint Stewart, Senior Data Scientist, University College London. Study lead;
the principal investigator is Joseph Lam. Primary adjudicator for all cases in
the adjudication population.

## Role in developing what is being adjudicated

* **The taxonomy.** I designed the Research Domains, Analytical Purposes and
  tags, and wrote their inclusion, exclusion and assignment rules, in
  collaboration with Claude (Anthropic). The categories were developed
  inductively from the register itself.
* **The classifier.** I wrote the classification prompt, in collaboration
  with Claude. I did not iterate the prompt against specific records at any
  point.
* **The dashboard.** I built it. It displays the model's classification for
  individual projects, but I have used it at the level of aggregate numbers;
  see the next section.
* **The scratch-coder study.** I wrote the coding instructions, trained the
  coders and built their REDCap instrument.
* **This adjudication instrument.** I designed the two-stage instrument, the
  rule reference and the mechanism vocabulary, with AI assistance, and
  piloted it on six permanently excluded pilot records.

Because I wrote the rules the model was instructed to apply, my reading of a
rule is also the author's reading. Where a rule is ambiguous, I may resolve it
toward what I intended rather than what it says. The rule reference records
the frozen text so a second reader can check.

## Prior exposure to formal records

* **Model outputs.** I have not examined the model's record-level
  classifications. My use of the dashboard has been at the level of aggregate
  numbers, apart from the first few records in its listing and incidental
  views while troubleshooting the enriched-register part of the dashboard.
  I would not recognise or remember any of these.
* **Coder classifications.** I have not seen record-level scratch-coder
  classifications for formal records.
* **Register entries.** Because the taxonomy was built inductively from the
  register, I will have read many register entries, and may have read some
  formal records' entries in passing. I would not recognise them at the level
  of individual projects.
* **Eligibility work.** The route-1 eligibility counts and the formal import
  were generated by scripts that printed aggregates only. I have seen no
  record-level eligibility, stratum or source information for formal records.
* **Pilot records.** I have examined the pilot records in detail and
  adjudicated six of them, including their revealed sources. They are
  permanently excluded from every validation analysis (§5.1).

Recognising a specific record's source during adjudication is recorded per
record as a masking failure (§9.3). This section covers general exposure only.

## Other relevant interests

None beyond those above.

## Safeguards in the design

Stated for the reader, not claimed as sufficient:

* Stage 1 is completed blind to which source produced each option, and is
  preserved before the reveal.
* A second reviewer independently reviews a random 20% of completed primary
  cases plus every case carrying a mandatory-review finding, without access to
  my assessments (§9.1). Joseph Lam, Research Fellow at UCL and the study's
  principal investigator, has agreed to act as secondary reviewer; final
  confirmation against the quoted workload is awaited. His own declaration of
  role and conflicts is recorded before he reviews any case.
* Differences are logged without forced consensus.
